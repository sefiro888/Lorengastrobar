Our story Che róga

From San José de los Arroyos to Camp de l'Arpa

Lorenza Vera —Loren to everyone— arrived in Spain more than 17 years ago with a suitcase full of recipes. She studied cooking, worked in restaurants and, in the middle of the pandemic, dared to open her own place to fulfil a dream:

"To prove that Paraguayan food is just as excellent as any other."— LOREN VERA

Today, together with her family —her son José Manuel is her "right hand"— and a team that will even serve you in Guaraní, Loren Restobar is a meeting point for Paraguayans who miss home and for locals discovering it for the first time.

Flavour dictionary tembi'u

First time trying Paraguayan food?

We'll make it easy. Hover over (or tap) each dish to find out what it is, how to say it and why no Paraguayan table is complete without it.

Chipa guazú

the star dish

Chipa guazú

chi-pa gua-SÚ

Baked cake of fresh sweetcorn, egg, onion and fresh cheese. Fluffy, golden and wheat-free.

"Guazú" means big in Guaraní

Sopa paraguaya

the soup you slice

Sopa paraguaya

so-pa pa-ra-GUA-ya

It's not a soup: it's a savoury cornbread made with cornmeal, curd, cheese and onion.

Legend has it that it was born from a "mistake" in President Carlos Antonio López's kitchen

Mbejú

crispy and cheesy

Mbejú

mbe-YÚ

Griddled flatbread made with cassava starch, cornmeal and cheese. Crispy outside, soft inside.

A Guaraní heritage; in Paraguay it's eaten with cocido or milky coffee

Vorí vorí

Ibai's favourite

Vorí vorí

bo-RÍ bo-RÍ

Free-range chicken broth with little balls of cornmeal and cheese. A hug in a bowl.

The dish Loren used to represent Paraguay in front of 1.7 million votes

Empanadas

the most talked-about

Empanadas

beef · chicken · ham & cheese · sweetcorn

Homemade pastry and a juicy filling. The beef one has hard-boiled egg, cumin and peppers, just as tradition demands.

The most mentioned dish in our Google reviews

Mandi'ó

cassava

Mandi'ó

man-di-Ó

Cassava (yuca). In Paraguay it goes with every meal like bread does here: boiled, fried "brava" style or sautéed as chyryry.

"Chyryry" imitates the sizzle of the frying pan

Mixed grill

on the brazier

Paraguayan mixed grill

the Sunday barbecue

Beef, chorizo, chinchulín and chicken, with cassava and chipa guazú, served sizzling on a brazier.

In Paraguay, a barbecue is almost a family ritual

Milanesa

ten different ways

Milanesa

a caballo · napolitana · fugazzeta

Beef or chicken breaded to order, in portions that overflow the plate. Also in a sandwich.

Order it "a caballo": topped with two fried eggs
