Our story Che róga

A suitcase full of recipes

This is the story of Lorenza Vera —Loren to everyone—, a cook from San José de los Arroyos who crossed the ocean with a dream: to prove that Paraguayan food is just as excellent as any other. Six years later, half of Barcelona knows what a chipa guazú is.

From the red earth to the City of Counts

Every dish at Loren Restobar has a story of hard work, family and pride behind it. This is ours.

01
PARAGUAY

Recipes are learnt by watching

Loren was born in San José de los Arroyos, in the heart of Paraguay. There, as in so many Paraguayan homes, cooking was passed down without books: by watching mothers and grandmothers knead chipa, curdle cheese and light the fire for the Sunday barbecue.

Those recipes from her parents and grandparents are still, to this day, the foundation of everything that leaves our kitchen.

"Our aim is that with one bite we can take our guests all the way to Paraguay."

02
MORE THAN 17 YEARS AGO

The leap across the ocean

Hoping to give her family a better future, Loren emigrated to Spain. At first she had to leave her children in Paraguay; she worked tirelessly and, in just one year, managed to bring them over.

She studied cooking, worked in restaurants and learnt the trade from the inside, without ever losing the flavours of home.

03
2020

Opening in the middle of a pandemic

While the world was closing, Loren opened. Together with her husband, José Manuel, she chose the name and set up Loren Gastrobar on Carrer de la Muntanya: a small place with 19 seats inside and 12 on the terrace, near Clot and Camp de l'Arpa metro stations.

Chipa guazú became the flagship of the house, and empanadas, sopa paraguaya and mbejú started travelling all over Barcelona thanks to delivery.

"It's hard, but I don't regret it."

04
2022

The family grows

On 28 October 2022, Loren Restobar was born: more tables, a bigger menu and a slightly more refined offer, with new stars such as mandi'ó chyryry and the mixed grill served on a brazier.

Today the whole Loren family welcomes you under that name at Carrer de la Muntanya 115, with the same home cooking as ever and a terrace that's full every weekend.

"My right hand is my son."

05
AUGUST 2026

Paraguay, at the Food World Cup

Streamer Ibai Llanos organised a "Food World Cup" decided by public vote, and the dish chosen to represent Paraguay was Loren's vorí vorí, which faced Uruguay's chivito in the round of 16.

Paraguay won on Instagram, the Paraguayan press got behind it and thousands of compatriots voted from all over the world. The chivito went through, but the pride stayed at home.

"We lost this time, but we're ready to come back."

06
24 · SEPTEMBER · 2026

Six years and counting

"These have been 6 years of great hope and constant hard work to bring you the best of Paraguayan cuisine in Barcelona." That's how Loren celebrated with those who make this little corner possible: the Paraguayan community that finds a piece of home here and the locals who discover it every day.

"Every Paraguayan who comes in brings a foreign friend."

Food World Cup round of 16

Vorí vorí vs chivito

Ten days of voting on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. Over 1.7 million votes in total. Paraguay won clearly on Instagram; Uruguay came out on top on TikTok and YouTube.

For Loren it was much more than a vote: "It was Paraguay united by its food."

Paraguay in Barcelona paraguayoite

A meeting point for the community

At weekends, many of our tables fill up with Paraguayan families who come from all over Barcelona —and from other cities— for their vorí vorí, their pot roast or the soyo with tortillita that reminds them of home.

We celebrate Guarania Day with the classic songs, Guaraní Language Day with teacher Mbo'ehára Marce, who came to check whether we'd serve him in Guaraní (spoiler: we did), and every national holiday with our shirts on.

And when someone tells us the food "tastes exactly like back home", we know we're on the right track.
