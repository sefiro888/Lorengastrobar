Flavours of Paraguay tembi'u paraguái

Paraguayan food, explained

Corn, cassava and cheese. With those three ingredients, Guaraní wisdom and centuries of cultural blending, Paraguay created one of the tastiest —and least known— cuisines in South America. Here it is, dish by dish.

The three pillars

It all starts with three words

Understand these three ingredients and you understand Paraguay. They're in almost everything we cook, and many of our classics are made without wheat.

Avati

Corn

Fresh (choclo) or as cornmeal. It's the base of chipa guazú, sopa paraguaya and the little balls in vorí vorí.

01

Chipa guazú

chi-pa gua-SÚ
The pride of every Paraguayan barbecue

A baked cake of fresh sweetcorn that stays fluffy inside and golden outside. "Guazú" means "big" in Guaraní: it's the tray-baked version of chipa.

How we make it
Fresh sweetcorn, onion, eggs, curd, soft cheese, lard and salt. Baked until golden.
How to eat it
Hot, in portions, as a starter or alongside the mixed grill.
Goes great with
Paraguayan mixed grill · Vorí vorí · an ice-cold Guaraná

Sopa paraguaya

so-pa pa-ra-GUA-ya
The only soup in the world you eat with a fork

Despite its name, it's a savoury cornbread. Legend has it that President Carlos Antonio López's cook added too much cornmeal to a soup… and the "mistake" became a national dish.

How we make it
Cornmeal, eggs, curd, soft cheese, onion, milk, lard, a hint of aniseed and salt.
How to eat it
Warm, on its own or with meat. You'll definitely want seconds.
Goes great with
Milanesa a caballo · Picada completa · Chipa guazú

Mbejú

mbe-YÚ
Crispy outside, stretchy cheese inside

A griddled flatbread made with cassava starch, a Guaraní heritage. Our reel showing how we make it got over 2,400 likes.

How we make it
Cassava starch, cornmeal, grated cheese, milk, butter and salt. The filling has melted mozzarella.
How to eat it
Freshly made. In Paraguay it's eaten at breakfast or teatime with cocido or milky coffee.

Vorí vorí

bo-RÍ bo-RÍ
The dish we represented Paraguay with

A beef or chicken broth with little balls of cornmeal and cheese. The name comes from "bolita" (little ball). With this dish Loren took Paraguay to Ibai Llanos' Food World Cup.

How we make it
Beef or chicken simmered slowly, vegetables and little cornmeal balls kneaded with cheese.
How to eat it
Piping hot, with a big spoon. In winter it's ordered more than ever.

        Chipa guazú, step by step

        Corn, cheese, onion and eggs, by hand and into the oven until the golden crust appears. Press play with the sound on and we'll tell you all about it from the kitchen.

        See it on the menu

        Homemade chipa guazú recipe

        Serves about 6. This is the traditional recipe to make at home; Loren's has its own secrets, and you'll find it on the menu.

        Prep: 20 min · Oven: 45 min · Serves 6

        Ingredients

        • 500 g sweet corn kernels (choclo), fresh or frozen
        • 1 large onion
        • 3 eggs
        • 250 g soft fresh cheese (in Paraguay, queso Paraguay)
        • 150 g curd cheese or ricotta
        • 200 ml milk
        • 50 g lard or butter
        • Salt

        Step by step

        1. Chop the onion and soften it in the lard over low heat, without browning.
        2. Blend the corn with the milk: creamy, but with some texture left.
        3. Beat the eggs and mix them with the corn, onion, crumbled cheese, curd and salt.
        4. Grease a baking dish, pour in the mixture and bake at 200 °C for about 40–45 minutes, until set with a golden crust.
        5. Let it rest for 10 minutes before cutting into portions. It's even better warm.

        Common questions

        What people always ask us

        The questions we get asked most at the bar about Paraguayan food.

        What is Paraguayan food?

        A cuisine of corn, cassava, cheese and meat born from the blend of Guaraní and Spanish traditions. Its best-known dishes are chipa guazú, sopa paraguaya, mbejú, vorí vorí, empanadas and milanesa.

        What's the difference between chipa and chipa guazú?

        Chipa is a small cassava-starch and cheese bread that Paraguayans eat for breakfast or on the go. Chipa guazú (“big chipa”) is something else: a bake of sweet corn, egg and cheese cooked in a dish and served in portions.

        Why isn't sopa paraguaya a soup?

        Because it's a savoury cornbread made with cornmeal, cheese, onion and milk, eaten with a fork. Legend has it that it was born from a mistake in the kitchen of President Carlos Antonio López, when too much flour went into a soup.

        What is vorí vorí and how do you say it?

        It's pronounced “boree boree”. It's a beef or chicken broth with little cornmeal and cheese dumplings, the dish Loren used to represent Paraguay in Ibai Llanos' Food World Cup. We make it at weekends, in limited quantities.

        Does Paraguayan food contain gluten?

        Many classics are made with corn and cassava instead of wheat, like chipa guazú, sopa paraguaya or mbejú. On the menu we mark them “Wheat-free*”, but they're made in a kitchen that also handles wheat: if you're coeliac or have an allergy, let us know before ordering.

        Where can I eat Paraguayan food in Barcelona?

        At Loren Restobar, Carrer de la Muntanya 115, in Camp de l'Arpa (metro L5 Camp de l'Arpa or L1/L2 Clot). We've been cooking homemade Paraguayan food since 2020, Wednesday to Sunday, and we also deliver through Glovo and Uber Eats. See our opening hours and directions.

        What should I order the first time?

        To start, chipa guazú, sopa paraguaya and a cheese-filled mbejú; then a beef empanada and a milanesa a caballo. If you're coming as a group, the picada completa. And if in doubt, check the full menu.

        What do people drink in Paraguay?

        The national drink is tereré: yerba mate with ice-cold water, shared at any time of day. With food, Guaraná and Pilsen, Paraguay's beer, are the favourites at Loren.
